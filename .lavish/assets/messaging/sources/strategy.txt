17  # Strategy
18  
19  ## Digital mission
20  
21  Help Brussels social-profit organisations and creative makers understand how to
22  apply to a Make a Mark edition, submit a useful application, and know what
23  happens next.
24  
25  This is a working synthesis of the five directions selected by the owner in
26  Lavish, not a separately approved tagline.
27  
28  ## Audiences and needs
29  
30  1. **Social-profit organisations and creative makers are co-primary applicants.**
31     The owner chose balanced framing because both apply and the team outcome
32     depends on both.
33  2. **Makers are the person-signup audience for this version.** A separate youth
34     signup is outside this scope until the 2027 youth role is defined.
35  3. **Young people remain important stakeholders** in the 2027 edition, but the
36     supplied project brief does not settle their participation route.
37  
38  The current needs model is provisional. Historical reporting suggests makers
39  may value local social impact and peer connection, while organisations may
40  need help turning communication challenges into a focused brief; one maker
41  described an earlier event as overwhelming. These observations are historical,
42  not representative current research. Validate the jobs, concerns, and language
43  with current applicants before treating them as facts. [Discovery synthesis;
44  BRUZZ report; 2020 Maker Packet]
45  
46  ## Product outcome
47  
48  The owner selected **review-ready applications across both tracks** as the
49  candidate North Star. Track maker and organisation counts separately by
50  edition. The current Airtable capture shows fields and linked records relevant
51  to applications and matching, but does not define what “review-ready” means;
52  Scope must define that criterion with the application reviewers. [Airtable data
53  model capture]
54  
55  ## Website role and lifecycle
56  
57  The website is an **edition-cycle guide**: it supports signup-open, signups-
58  closed, and after-event states for an edition. During signup it serves separate
59  maker and organisation applications. When signups are closed it must explain
60  what happens to applicants. After the event it should show results and offer
61  future interest. Signup administration in the website is explicitly later.
62  The supplied forms and Airtable record model establish two application tracks,
63  edition relationships, a past organisation future-interest form, and maker
64  future-interest records; they do not establish current phase switching or
65  submission behavior. [Owner brief; form capture; data model capture]
66  
67  ## Success and limits
68  
69  Near-term success is review-ready applications received by the edition deadline,
70  tracked separately for makers and organisations. Attendance, useful outputs,
71  and impact matter to the programme but are not attributable to the website
72  alone. No target values or reviewer definition are yet established.
73  
74  This version uses the Airtable model as its migration starting point, imports
75  all Airtable content into Laravel, and retires Airtable as the ongoing system.
76  A website admin for managing signups is out of scope for the first version.
77  Consent, retention, edition-phase rules, exact form requiredness, and
78  submission confirmations remain Scope decisions. [Owner migration direction;
79  Airtable form capture; Airtable data model capture]
80  
81  ## Decisions recorded
82  
83  - Person signup: makers only.
84  - Priority: organisations and makers are co-primary.
85  - Product role: edition-cycle guide.
86  - Candidate North Star: review-ready applications across both tracks.
87  - After-event priority: show results and invite future interest.
