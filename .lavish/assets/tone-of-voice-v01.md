# Tone of voice guide

Make a Mark Brussels, draft v0.1. This is a proposed writing reference, not an approved brand policy. All rules and new examples below are editorial recommendations. Source observations are labelled separately. Strategy and its audience priorities remain open in the [concerns register](01-strategy-concerns.md).

## 1. Tone of voice statement

**We speak warmly, plainly, and with creative energy, inviting people to make useful things together for Brussels and explaining what taking part involves.**

This 23-word statement is a proposal. Think of the voice as a thoughtful event host who knows both the creative work and the people around the table. Welcome people, explain the work, and make the next step clear.

Evidence behind this direction: the existing site uses informal invitations and lively creative language; the 2027 brief describes volunteer creative support; historical local materials emphasise welcome, preparation, and concrete outputs. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) [E6](#sources)

## 2. Voice attributes

### Readers and their context

The application captures address makers and organisations separately; the organisation contact form serves people interested in a future edition. [E3](#sources) Historical BRUZZ reporting describes makers seeking meaningful use of their skills and connection with peers, while organisations describe limited time or resources for design and marketing. [E5](#sources) Interpretation: explain the value of participation and the work it requires without making people decode creative jargon. These are historical audience hypotheses pending current research in D-002, not validated needs for every applicant. Audience ranking remains open in S-002.

These qualities stay constant. The amount of playfulness and detail changes with the situation. Confidence describes the evidence for the recommendation, not owner approval.

| Attribute | How to write | Avoid | Proposed example | Evidence and confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | Address the reader as a person. Thank people for their time. | Insider banter that assumes everyone already belongs. | "Thanks for sharing your work with us." | Existing conversational FAQs and historical maker welcome. Medium. [E1](#sources) [E6](#sources) |
| Direct | State the action and what it means before adding colour. | Hiding unpaid work or selection behind a joke. | "Apply to take part as a maker." | Application forms distinguish applying from participating. Medium. [E3](#sources) [E4](#sources) |
| Collaborative | Give organisations and makers an active role. Name what each brings. | Presenting makers as rescuers or organisations as passive recipients. | "Your organisation brings the challenge. Makers bring creative skills." | Organisation briefings, team relationships, and historical planning meetings. Medium. [E3](#sources) [E4](#sources) [E6](#sources) |
| Creative | Use lively verbs and the occasional playful line. Describe actual work. | Vague claims about changing the world or forced creative slang. | "Bring your skills. Make something useful for Brussels." | Existing headline and playful maker copy; current brief names design, communication, and digital challenges. Medium. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) |
| Grounded | Describe the contribution and its limits. Attribute outcomes to evidence. | Guaranteed transformation, finished products, or future support. | "A day of creative work on a challenge your organisation brings." | One-day format in the brief; historical testimonials describe specific outputs, not proven long-term impact. Medium. [E2](#sources) [E6](#sources) |
| Brussels-rooted | Name Brussels and the people or work involved when relevant. | Generic charity language or claims to speak for the whole city. | "Creative skills for organisations working with young people in Brussels." | Brussels focus in the site and 2027 brief. Medium. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) |

## 3. Language and style

Proposed rules, medium confidence overall. Language coverage, naming, and playfulness need owner choices TOV-001 to TOV-004.

| Rule | Use | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name the actor. | "We select makers before the event." | "A selection procedure will be undertaken." | Readers can see who does what. Selection appears in the forms and site. [E1](#sources) [E3](#sources) |
| Give one next step. | "Apply as a maker." | "Join the movement and unlock your impact." | A button should explain its action. Application and future-interest paths are distinct. [E3](#sources) [E4](#sources) |
| Put the practical answer first. | "Makers volunteer their time and skills." | "Kind of. In drinks, delicious snacks and good vibes." as the only answer about payment. | Keep the site's warmth while making unpaid participation clear. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) |
| Describe useful work. | "Work on a visual identity or communication challenge." | "Make organisations look fabulous." as the full value proposition. | Appearance alone undersells the work described in the brief and organisation applications. [E2](#sources) [E3](#sources) |
| Use everyday English for this draft. | "Tell us what your organisation needs help with." | "Submit your organisational capacity-building requirements." | The current site is in English. Audience language preferences remain unvalidated. [E1](#sources) [E3](#sources) |
| Keep enthusiasm proportionate. | "We'd love to see your work." | "Calling all creative superheroes!!!" | This is a proposed restraint, not a discovered brand ban. It supports the collaborative framing above. |
| Be precise about participation. | "Apply to take part." | "You're in!" before acceptance. | Application, selection, team assignment, and future interest are different states in the model. [E4](#sources) |
| Make commitments visible. | "Check the time commitment before applying." | "Just give us a day." | Historical forms include preparation and planning commitments beyond the event day. Confirm the current schedule before listing it. [E3](#sources) [E6](#sources) |

Use "we" for the organising team and "you" for the reader. Say "makers and organisations" when both groups are meant; do not let an ambiguous "we" erase their roles. Prefer short paragraphs, sentence case, descriptive links, and natural contractions. Avoid unexplained acronyms, decorative punctuation, and emoji in instructions. These are proposed writing defaults, not observed house rules.

### Inclusion and young people

Proposed principle: describe young people as people with perspectives and agency. Avoid pity, saviour language, invented youth slang, or claims that we speak for them. The 2027 brief calls for young people's involvement, but their participation route and preferred language are unresolved. Do not publish a youth signup invitation until S-001 is resolved. [E2](#sources) [S-001](01-strategy-concerns.md)

English is the drafting language because it matches the current site. The 2027 brief calls for a website in Dutch, French, and English for grant applications, and its meeting notes discuss that proposal. The application captures also ask about those languages. These sources show multilingual intent, without confirming website scope, a primary language, or event language guarantees. Proposed translation defaults, subject to TOV-004: keep the same practical information across languages and use natural local phrasing. Do not assign Dutch or French forms of address before review. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) [E3](#sources)

### Facts and promises

Before publishing, check edition facts against the current edition source. The owner confirmed **27 March 2027**. The venue and detailed format remain unresolved; older application dates, capacity, meeting times, meals, and deadlines are historical. [E2](#sources) [E3](#sources) [D-004](../discovery/01-concerns.md)

Do not invent response times, acceptance criteria, accessibility provisions, language support, confidentiality, consent terms, ongoing support, or the right to use someone's story or image. The available forms and data model do not establish current rules for these. Raise missing facts for confirmation. [E3](#sources) [E4](#sources) [D-005](../discovery/01-concerns.md)

## 4. Terminology

Proposed defaults, medium confidence for observed names and low confidence for editorial choices. TOV-003 covers organisation naming.

| Prefer | Avoid or qualify | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make a Mark Brussels on first mention; Make a Mark after | Invented abbreviations such as MAM in public copy | Matches the site's name and short name. [E1](#sources) |
| maker / makers in prose; Maker in an existing official title | Creatives as the only explanation of who can apply | Proposed casing. Explain makers as people volunteering creative skills; confirm eligible roles before listing them. The current site capitalises Makers. [E1](#sources) [E3](#sources) |
| social organisation on first mention; organisation after | Beneficiary, needy organisation, client | Editorial choice. Existing sources vary between social-profits, social organisations, and nonprofits. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) [E6](#sources) |
| make-a-thon, explained as a day of collaborative creative work | Hackathon without explanation | Uses the event's existing term while explaining it for newcomers. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) |
| apply / application for selection | Register / book your place before acceptance | Avoid suggesting a guaranteed place. [E3](#sources) [E4](#sources) |
| Hear when applications open for future interest | Apply now when signup is closed | Names the separate action shown in the historical contact form. Use only when that flow exists. [E3](#sources) |
| volunteer time and skills | Free labour; unpaid hidden behind refreshments | Names the contribution directly. [E1](#sources) [E2](#sources) |
| specific outputs, attributed participant feedback | Proven impact; life-changing; guaranteed results | Two testimonials express appreciation and hopes. They do not establish measured long-term outcomes. [E6](#sources) |
| young people; organisations working with young people | Our youth; the voiceless; youth slang used to sound relatable | Proposed respectful wording. The 2027 youth focus does not establish a direct signup audience. [E2](#sources) |

## 5. Channel and situation adaptations

The voice stays warm, direct, collaborative, creative, grounded, and Brussels-rooted. Adjust energy and detail to what the reader needs. This matrix is a proposal, low confidence: the source set does not contain a representative sample for every channel.

| Channel / situation | Tone and style | Address | Proposed example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website introduction | Warm, lively, plain language. Explain the event before the invitation. | You; makers and organisations | "Make a Mark brings volunteer makers and Brussels social organisations together for a day of creative work." |
| Application form | Calm and exact. Explain what is needed and why, one step at a time. | You / your organisation | "Tell us about the creative challenge your organisation wants to work on." |
| Social invitation | More energy. One concrete idea and one action; a playful line can lead. | You | "Brussels has work to do. Bring your creative skills." |
| Participant email / internal coordination | Collegial and practical. Put dates, responsibilities, and the next step first. | You / we with a named actor | "Before the planning meeting, gather the files your team will need." |
| Selection or rejection email | Personal, clear, restrained. State the decision; thank the person. | You | "Thank you for applying. We can't offer you a place this edition." |
| Formal partner or funder communication | Professional, concrete. Describe scope and cite evidence for outcomes. | Make a Mark Brussels / we | "The 2027 edition focuses on Brussels organisations working with young people." |
| Closed applications / after the event | Clear about current status. Offer only an available next action. | You | "Applications are closed for this edition." |
| Form error | Calm and useful. Explain what to fix without blame or jokes. | You only if needed | "Enter an email address in the format name@example.org." |

Examples are newly written illustrations grounded in the site, brief, application captures, and historical coordination materials. They are not approved copy or proof that these flows exist. The participant email example applies only if that preparation step is confirmed. Future-interest and after-event calls to action depend on S-005 and D-005. ## 6. Current voice: keep and refine

These eight excerpts are observed copy from the migrated site, not a sample of current social posts. [Site copy](../../../resources/content/site.json) and [FAQ copy](../../../resources/content/faq/01-paid.json) sit beside the [baseline capture](../../raw/2026-10-18-astro-site.md).

| Existing excerpt | Assessment and proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| "Hundreds of creatives. One day. Brussels." | Keep the short rhythm and local focus. Treat "hundreds" as a historical claim to verify, not an edition attendance promise. |
| "Make a Mark Brussels is a make-a-thon." | Direct, but explain the event for someone who has never heard the term. |
| "look fabulous" | Warm and playful. Follow with what the creative work helps the organisation do. |
| "turbo creative mode" | Distinctive energy. Test in promotional copy; replace with plain wording in practical instructions. |
| "We select Makers in advance" | Useful transparency. Follow with confirmed timing and next steps for the edition. |
| "Please make sure you can make it." | Human but vague. State the specific commitment once confirmed. |
| "Kind of. In drinks, delicious snacks and good vibes." | Warm, but indirect about payment. Proposed rewrite: "Makers volunteer their time and skills." |
| "No strings attached." | Generous, but too broad for participation that involves preparation. Proposed rewrite: "Organisations take part without a participation fee." Publish only after confirming the current terms. |

The last two excerpts come from the [maker payment FAQ](../../../resources/content/faq/01-paid.json) and [organisation payment FAQ](../../../resources/content/faq/02-organisations-pay.json). Their claims describe the existing site, not verified 2027 arrangements.

| TOV-001 | Overall character. Sources combine informal welcome with practical coordination. | **Warm host:** welcoming and straightforward, with creative energy. | **Creative rally:** punchier invitations and bolder verbs. **Practical partner:** quieter, more explanatory copy. Changes rhythm and emphasis, not eligibility or audience rank. |
| TOV-002 | Playfulness. The site uses humour; no approved boundary exists. | **Playful invitations, plain practical copy.** | **Playfulness throughout:** light warmth in explanations, still clear on costs and selection. **Plain throughout:** remove playful phrases. | TOV-005 | The youth focus is clear; young people's own route is unresolved in S-001. | **Explain the focus without a direct youth invitation.** | **Add a youth-facing sample as a hypothesis:** test respectful direct address, explicitly pending participation decisions and review with young people. This does not resolve S-001. |

## 8. Applying the guide

For a writer or AI: use this draft's six attributes and chosen terminology. Start with the reader's question. State the concrete next step. Keep applications distinct from acceptance and future interest. In sensitive or practical copy, prioritise clarity. Check every logistical claim against current edition evidence. Mark missing facts for the owner rather than filling them in.

Before approving a piece of copy, check that it names the action, respects both makers and organisations, explains the commitment, and avoids unsupported outcomes. Apply the channel row that matches the situation. Do not use this draft to settle open Strategy decisions.

### Confidence and research limits

| Section | Confidence | What would strengthen it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statement and attributes | Medium | Owner review and reactions from current makers and organisations. |
| Language and style | Medium | Confirm the desired playfulness and practical information for 2027. |
| Naming and multilingual guidance | Low for recommendations | TOV-003 and TOV-004, plus fluent language review. |
| Channel adaptations | Low | Current emails, partner communication, and a representative social sample. |
| Existing copy analysis | High for the quoted site text only | It establishes wording, not effectiveness or audience preference. |

This version uses all six product source captures and the Discovery synthesis, plus the migrated site's wording and the retrieved playbook/template. It does not claim a fresh website audit, a review of 10–15 social posts, transcripts, or direct audience research. Historical Brussels materials and the international field guide are contextual evidence, not current policy. Proposed anti-patterns are editorial advice, not experimentally proven failures.
