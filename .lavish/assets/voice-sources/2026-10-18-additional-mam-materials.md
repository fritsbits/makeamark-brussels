# Additional Make a Mark materials — source capture

- Sources: six files supplied by the owner in `.bloom/attachments/` on 2026-10-18.
- Capture method: PDF text extraction where available, and visual reading of the testimonial images. The notes below summarize the supplied materials; they do not reproduce them verbatim.
- Source distinction: the Official Make-a-Thon Field Guide is an international resource from the broader Make a Mark movement, not a local Brussels document. The other items describe or quote historical Brussels editions.

## Brussels Maker Packet — Aprèstoe, 2020

File: `.bloom/attachments/EejPrR/Copy of MAM_BXL_2020 - Maker Packet - aprèstoe.pdf`.

This packet is for a selected maker working with Aprèstoe. It welcomes makers and explains that their time will be protected and work valued. It introduces the organisation, its project, its contact, and a shared folder for source and final files. It explains the pre-event planning meeting, what makers should expect and bring, the event schedule and food/logistics. A pledge asks participants to attend the meeting and event, coordinate needed assets, complete work by the end of the event, and hand over original files. Separate signature sections address permission to use the work and photo/video use.

UX relevance: this is evidence of a selected-maker onboarding and expectation-setting step after application, not part of the public signup form. Its 2020 date and agreement language are historical and should not be treated as current legal policy.

## Official Make-a-Thon Field Guide — international resource

File: `.bloom/attachments/7TeGsK/Official Make-a-Thon Field Guide.pdf`.

The guide describes the broader movement's model and suggested practices. Its application section says maker applications gather skills, experience, and project preferences; organisation applications gather mission, impact, need, and a proposed project. It allows local organisers to open both forms together or open organisation applications first. The guide describes closing applications, interviews and selection, acceptance/rejection messages, optional maker RSVPs, team formation, planning meetings, event delivery, thank-you messages, file checks, and a post-event feedback survey. It presents planning meetings as both functional (project details and preparation) and relational (meeting collaborators and building trust).

UX relevance: this suggests a fuller lifecycle than the website signup itself and identifies moments where people need clear status and next steps. These are international recommendations/examples, not evidence that Brussels currently follows or wants every practice in this guide.

## Brussels Make a Mark Recap — 2019

File: `.bloom/attachments/VasESN/Copy of Make a Mark Brussels 2019 Recap. Brussels, Belgium makes their mark on… _ by Sarah Obenauer _ Make a Mark _ Medium.pdf`.

The article recounts the first Brussels Make a Mark event outside the United States. It describes makers travelling from Antwerp, Ghent, and London; the event welcome and meals; makers choosing work areas; an organisation check-in; workshops; a deadline extension; an evening showcase at a second venue; and follow-up photos and video. These details are a historical account of one local edition, not a current operations plan.

## Brussels nonprofit introductions — 2019

File: `.bloom/attachments/WzvEEM/Copy of Meet the Brussels 2019 Nonprofits. Make a Mark Selects 14 Nonprofits _ by Sarah Obenauer _ Make a Mark _ Medium.pdf`.

The article introduces fourteen selected Brussels organisations and their missions before the 2019 event. It gives examples across employment, poverty, community spaces, culture, mobility, mental health, youth, family support, and other local needs. It frames the make-a-thon as a one-day opportunity to connect organisations with creative support. This is historical participant-facing editorial content, not an application schema or evidence about current eligibility.

## Organisation testimonials — Brussels, 2023

Files: `.bloom/attachments/UgQyef/testimonial BAOB BRUSSELS LinkedIn - MamBXL 2023.png` and `.bloom/attachments/LZVhnw/testimonial 100 PAP LinkedIn - MamBXL 2023.pdf`.

The BAOB Brussels post by Juliette Berguet describes a new chapter beginning with Make a Mark Brussels' help, thanks collaborators for a positive connection, and says it was the organisation's first hackathon and would not be its last.

The 100PAP post describes joy at being selected, excitement at seeing early ideas, and happiness at receiving tools to give the organisation new momentum. It says a team worked for twelve hours on the organisation's identity, including a logo, photography, visuals, and slogans, and praises the amount and quality of volunteer work. It hopes the work will help more undocumented people find housing.

These are public testimonials from two organisations. They support hypotheses about selection, anticipation, tangible output, and social impact, but do not represent all participants or establish longer-term outcomes.

## Limits

Most materials describe 2019, 2020, or 2023. They are useful historical evidence about local touchpoints and how participants described the experience, but dates, venues, event details, agreements, process rules, and expectations must be confirmed for the 2027 edition. The international field guide is explicitly not local policy. The Airtable schema remains uninspected.
