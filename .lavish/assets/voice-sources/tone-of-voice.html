1. Tone of voice statement

We speak warmly, plainly, and with creative energy, inviting people to make useful things together for Brussels and explaining what taking part involves.

This 23-word statement remains candidate wording. The owner chose the warm host direction in TOV-001. Write like a thoughtful event host who knows both the creative work and the people around the table. Welcome people, explain the work, and make the next step clear. Owner decision

Evidence behind this direction: the existing site uses informal invitations and lively creative language; the 2027 brief describes volunteer creative support; historical local materials emphasise welcome, preparation, and concrete outputs. E1 E2 E6

3. Language and style

The owner chose playful invitations and plain practical copy in TOV-002. Use creative language in invitations; give direct answers in instructions, forms, costs, selection, errors, and rejection messages. Plain practical copy can still be kind. How much welcoming or appreciative wording to include is being tested in TOV-010 and TOV-011. The detailed rules below remain editorial recommendations, supported by medium evidence confidence. Owner decisions

Rule Use Avoid Why
Name the actor. "We select makers before the event." "A selection procedure will be undertaken." Readers can see who does what. Selection appears in the forms and site. E1 E3
Give one next step. "Apply as a maker." "Join the movement and unlock your impact." A button should explain its action. Application and future-interest paths are distinct. E3 E4
Put the practical answer first. "Makers volunteer their time and skills." "Kind of. In drinks, delicious snacks and good vibes." as the only answer about payment. Keep the site's warmth while making unpaid participation clear. E1 E2
Describe useful work. "Work on a visual identity or communication challenge." "Make organisations look fabulous." as the full value proposition. Appearance alone undersells the work described in the brief and organisation applications. E2 E3
Use everyday English for this draft. "Tell us what your organisation needs help with." "Submit your organisational capacity-building requirements." The current site is in English. Audience language preferences remain unvalidated. E1 E3
Keep enthusiasm proportionate. "We'd love to see your work." "Calling all creative superheroes!!!" This is a proposed restraint, not a discovered brand ban. It supports the collaborative framing above.
Be precise about participation. "Apply to take part." "You're in!" before acceptance. Application, selection, team assignment, and future interest are different states in the model. E4
Make commitments visible. "Check the time commitment before applying." "Just give us a day." Historical forms include preparation and planning commitments beyond the event day. Confirm the current schedule before listing it. E3 E6

Use "we" for the organising team and "you" for the reader. Say "makers and organisations" when both groups are meant; do not let an ambiguous "we" erase their roles. Prefer short paragraphs, sentence case, descriptive links, and natural contractions. Avoid unexplained acronyms, decorative punctuation, and emoji in instructions. These are proposed writing defaults, not observed house rules.

5. Channel and situation adaptations

The voice stays warm, direct, collaborative, creative, grounded, and Brussels-rooted. Adjust energy and detail to what the reader needs. This matrix is a proposal, low confidence: the source set does not contain a representative sample for every channel.

Channel / situation Tone and style Address Proposed example
Website introduction Warm, lively, plain language. Explain the event before the invitation. You; makers and organisations "Make a Mark brings volunteer makers and Brussels social organisations together for a day of creative work."
Application form Calm and exact. Explain what is needed and why, one step at a time. You / your organisation "Tell us about the creative challenge your organisation wants to work on."
Social invitation More energy. One concrete idea and one action; a playful line can lead. You "Brussels has work to do. Bring your creative skills."
Participant email / internal coordination Collegial and practical. Put dates, responsibilities, and the next step first. You / we with a named actor "Before the planning meeting, gather the files your team will need."
Selection or rejection email Personal, clear, restrained. State the decision; thank the person. You "Thank you for applying. We can't offer you a place this edition."
Formal partner or funder communication Professional, concrete. Describe scope and cite evidence for outcomes. Make a Mark Brussels / we "The 2027 edition focuses on Brussels organisations working with young people."
Closed applications / after the event Clear about current status. Offer only an available next action. You "Applications are closed for this edition."
Form error Calm and useful. Explain what to fix without blame or jokes. You only if needed "Enter an email address in the format name@example.org."

8. Applying the guide

For a writer or AI: lead with the confirmed warm host voice. Keep playful language in invitations and practical answers plain. Use social organisation first and organisation after. Draft in English and explain the youth focus without a direct youth invitation. Use this draft's six attributes as supporting editorial guidance. Start with the reader's question. State the concrete next step. Keep applications distinct from acceptance and future interest. In sensitive or practical copy, prioritise clarity. Check every logistical claim against current edition evidence. Mark missing facts for the owner rather than filling them in.

Before approving a piece of copy, check that it names the action, respects both makers and organisations, explains the commitment, and avoids unsupported outcomes. Apply the channel row that matches the situation. Keep copy choices consistent with the validated Strategy; they do not change product scope or confirm operational details.
