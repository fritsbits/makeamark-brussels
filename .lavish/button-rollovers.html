R01 · Recommended

Let the arrow lead.

The arrow nudges 3px toward the destination over 180ms. The words, outline and background stay still.

This keeps the button easy to read on both red and cream. It would replace the current colour inversion on these navigation pills.

Tradeoff. A quiet signal. It relies on an arrow for movement, so navigation pills without one would gain the same small icon.

Hover, use Tab, or tap a demo to hold its rollover state.
R02 · More expressive

Sweep the ink across.

A solid layer sweeps from left to right over 260ms. Its contrasting label travels with the new background as one clipped layer, while the words stay in exactly the same position.

On red, cream ink reveals a red label. On cream, red ink reveals a cream-light label. Both colour pairs have at least 4.78:1 contrast.

Tradeoff. The boldest treatment needs two perfectly aligned label layers. The duplicate is hidden from assistive technology. There is no independent colour fade that could wash the text out halfway.

Move in and out quickly to test an interrupted sweep.
R03 · More playful

Send one arrow. Bring one back.

The arrow leaves through the top-right corner of its small window as another arrives from the bottom left, over 240ms. The label, outline and background do not move.

It suggests following a link without turning the whole button into an animation.

Tradeoff. More character than R01, with slightly more visual activity. I'd use it consistently on navigation pills rather than mixing it with R01.

Only the icon changes. The label remains in place.
