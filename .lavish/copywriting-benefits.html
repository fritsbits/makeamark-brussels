Make it clear
who gets to make
a difference.

Makers want to put creative skills to meaningful use and meet their peers. Social organisations want a maker team’s help to move a long-waiting project forward. The copy can name both benefits—and show who does what.

The title gap

The current headlines point toward the event, but leave some of its value for the body copy to explain. The organisation title names a challenge without naming the maker team’s role.

Current homepage

One day.
Many ways
to make a mark.

Memorable, but the audience and useful exchange are not explicit in the headline.

Current audience titles

Makers: “A day for your creative skills.”

A warm invitation, but it could name the meaningful project and peer connection sooner.

Organisations: “Bring one real communication challenge.”

Names what the organisation brings, but not the creative help it receives.

Make the exchange visible

Keep makers and social organisations equally present on the homepage. On each audience page, lead with the benefit that matters most to that reader.

Homepage · recommended starting point

Make something useful together.

Makers bring creative skills. Social organisations bring a challenge worth taking on. Together, they make practical work for Brussels.

A shared headline keeps both audiences in view; the supporting sentence explains their distinct contributions. “Practical work” avoids guaranteeing a finished deliverable.

For makers

Pair contribution and connection: use your skills for a worthwhile project, alongside other makers.

Recommended

Put your creative skills to work on a project that matters.

Spend a day making something useful with a Brussels social organisation—and meet other makers while you do.

States purpose in the title, then brings peer connection into the first supporting sentence.

More concrete

Make useful work with a Brussels social organisation.

Bring your creative skills to a real communication or design challenge, alongside other makers.

Explains the activity quickly. Less emotional, and peer connection becomes secondary.

For social organisations

Make the handoff clear: the organisation brings the project; makers take on the creative work and help bring it forward.

Recommended

Get a maker team to bring a long-waiting project to life.

Bring a communication or design project you’ve been waiting to take on. Makers will help shape it into a practical direction or first materials.

Names the benefit and the team doing the creative work, while keeping the deliverable modest.

More direct

Let a maker team take on your communication challenge.

Tell us what your organisation has been meaning to make. We’ll match it with makers who can help shape the work.

Plain and action-oriented. “Take on” may sound like the makers work independently, so the lead should retain organisation input and feedback.

Keep the promise in bounds

The current page says teams work towards a “practical direction or first materials.” Keep that scope until 2027 outcomes are confirmed. The project brief mentions usable results, but its source capture is marked unverified.

Why this direction

The maker and organisation motivations are supported by historical evidence and owner direction. Treat them as useful hypotheses, not proven preferences for every current applicant.

Makers

Meaningful use + creative company

A 2023 BRUZZ report describes participants using their skills for socially meaningful work, meeting like-minded people, and feeling part of a close-knit community.

Historical report from one edition; current audience research could change the emphasis.

Organisations

Creative help for work on hold

The report describes organisations with limited time or resources for design and marketing. The owner’s recent feedback sharpens the invitation: they want a maker team to make or develop a project they have waited to pursue.

Historical evidence plus direct owner direction. Avoid implying a guaranteed final product.

Which wording feels right?

Selections stay local until you queue your answer. Choose the recommended title, the alternative, or write a new direction for each audience. You can mix and match.
