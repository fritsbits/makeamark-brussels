H01Give the group photo the stage.

Proposed. Put “A feel for the day.” above the photograph and the resource links below. Let the whole photograph grow from 75% to full content width as it enters view, without cropping anyone out.

The scroll controls the growth. It finishes before the image reaches the viewport centre. Scrolling back reverses it naturally; the page never pins or takes over scrolling.

Current layout: homepage template, evidence section. Current interaction: photo reveal source. Scale and timing are proposed values.

Why I recommend it. Today the group photo shares a row with the links. A dedicated, wide photograph makes the Brussels community the focal point.

Tradeoff. A taller section. The resource links follow the photo rather than sitting alongside it.

On phones. A gentler 90% to 100% growth. Reduced-motion users see the full-size photo immediately. The existing colour reveal stays separate.

H02Turn the opening into a poster.

Proposed. Let the headline take more of the opening, with deliberate line breaks and a desktop size around 150px. Keep the existing copy and fonts. Nest the photographs at its edge rather than giving text and images two equally contained columns.

Borrow the confidence of the global site's oversized lettering. Use one readable headline here; the reference's repeated lettering is inspiration for scale.

Current copy and composition: homepage template. Current type cap of 118px: type tokens. Reference: global resources page.

Why I recommend it. A larger headline makes the opening read like an event invitation and sets up the later photo moment.

Tradeoff. A stronger editorial composition needs specific line breaks at wide, medium and phone widths. The photos must not obscure the words or the application status.

The mockup uses the temporary global-community photograph already on the homepage. The separate decision to replace international photos with sourced Brussels photos still applies.

H03Make both invitations unmistakable.

Proposed. Increase both audience headings, give each more space, and add the same outward arrow to their actions. Keep the two routes equally prominent and preserve the current words.

Both audiences should immediately recognise where they belong, even after a more expressive hero.

Audience words and destinations: homepage audience panels. The darker cream and pill buttons come from the existing system.

My view. Useful if H02 goes ahead. The audience choices need enough presence to follow the larger headline.

Tradeoff. Taller panels, especially for “For social organisations”. Phones stack the choices at equal width.

H04A little motion around the edges.

Proposed. On first load, the hero photos settle upward by 20px with an 80ms stagger. Headline, reading copy and actions are immediately available. On hover, link arrows move a few pixels toward their destination.

The group photo remains the main scroll animation. Other sections stay steady.

Existing tilt, photos and colour interaction: photo reveal. These entrance and arrow movements are new proposals.

Optional. I'd review H01 and H02 first. This adds polish but contributes less than the larger compositional changes.

Tradeoff. Motion can compete with the photo reveal. Keep entrance movement on a separate outer wrapper and disable it for reduced-motion users.

What to borrow from the global site.

My interpretation. Its strongest contribution here is scale: oversized condensed lettering, broad cream and red fields, and photography given room to dominate.

Brussels already has the palette, type character and monochrome photography to push in that direction. These suggestions use its current system. Texture can wait until the larger composition works.
