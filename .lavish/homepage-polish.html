P01Recommended

Bring the audience choices closer.

Observed. The hero ends with 88px of padding; the audience section starts with another 96px. Mobile adds 48px + 56px. Source: 387, 408

Proposed. Use one shared pause: 80px of combined padding on desktop, 48px on mobile. Keep the headline, collage, panels and section order.

My recommendation: the second invitation should feel connected to the first. The current blank band interrupts that handoff.

184px to 80px

Tradeoff. Less airy. This changes only the hero-to-audience transition, not every section on the page.

P02Recommended

Make the evidence look clickable.

Observed. The BRUZZ and Instagram links render without an underline. At rest, they look like the surrounding body copy. Source: 60–67; CSS 19

Proposed. Use two underlined resource rows, a restrained rule and an outward arrow. Keep the exact link wording and destinations.

My recommendation: this is the clearest gain for the least visual disruption. The supporting evidence becomes easier to recognise.

Visible links at rest

Tradeoff. Adds two hairlines and a little more structure beside the photo. The photo treatment stays as selected.

P03Taste / optional

Give each organiser a readable row.

Observed. Names and roles run together on one line, then wrap at different places on mobile. Source: 71–81; CSS 220–227

Proposed. Align names and roles in two columns on desktop. Stack the role beneath the name on mobile, with quiet rules between people.

My interpretation: the people are worth a little more care. This makes the role easier to read without adding biographies.

A choice of density

Tradeoff. A taller list on phones. The existing handwritten logo still meets the footer.

P04Recommended

Finish the mobile menu states.

Observed. The menu control measures 40 × 40px. It keeps the hamburger glyph while the menu is open. Source: 1–9; CSS 296–302

Proposed. Enlarge the control to 48 × 48px and turn the open-state glyph into a close mark. Preserve the existing Escape and link-close behaviour.

My recommendation: a clearer open state and more room to tap. This is a usability refinement, with very little new visual language.

40px to 48px

Tradeoff. The mobile header grows by 8px. This is a shared header change, so verify the audience and application pages too.

Small shared-nav change

P05Taste / optional

Let the footer breathe in groups.

Observed. Seven navigation links share one wrapping row on desktop. Their rendered link boxes are about 19px tall. Source: 1–21; CSS 266–276

Proposed. Separate audience/about links, community links, and privacy/contact links into simple groups. Give each link at least 44px of height.

My interpretation: the closing section feels compressed after such a generous page. Grouping makes the next destination easier to scan.

One row to three groups

Tradeoff. A deeper footer. This affects all public pages and needs a check beside the logo that rests on its top edge. Target size is a comfort improvement; this review does not establish a WCAG failure.

P06Recommended

Match the invitation to its destination.

Observed. “Choose your application” jumps back to the audience panels. Those panels lead to information pages before the forms. Source: 48–51, 28–38

Proposed. Rename this link to “Explore ways to take part”. Keep its destination at #take-part and keep both audiences equally prominent.

My recommendation: name the next step accurately. The application actions remain on the audience pages.

Same route, clearer label

Tradeoff. Less direct application language. This is a wording decision for you, not an automatic correction to approved copy.

P07Taste / optional

Offer a quiet hint for the photo reveal.

Observed. Photos already reveal colour on hover, tap and keyboard activation. The captions do not explain that interaction. Source: 3–10; CSS 310–331

Proposed. Add a small “Tap a photo for colour” cue for touch input, beside the existing attribution. Use a hover cue for a fine pointer.

Optional: this helps people discover the reveal you selected. I would keep the current quietness unless discovery matters more.

Optional discovery cue

Tradeoff. More caption text around an intentionally understated interaction. The monochrome default, reveal animation and reduced-motion behaviour stay intact.
