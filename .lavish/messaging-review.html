Observation

The same explanation in five places.

The hero, process, organisation panel, edition text and About intro all explain the collaboration again. Each adds little to that basic explanation. [1–4]

Hero

“brings creative makers and Brussels social organisations together”

Organisation panel

“Work alongside a team of makers on a communication, design, or digital challenge”

Edition block

“brings volunteer makers together with Brussels social organisations for one day of creative work”

Interpretation: the sequence feels busy because several large headings restart the pitch. Shorter sentences alone would leave that pattern intact.

Editorial recommendations · awaiting your review

Make these four cuts.

These keep the two equal applicant routes and the existing page boundaries. They change how much each section says. The agreed structure explicitly left content reduction open. [5–6]

D01

Combine the youth and edition blocks.

Use one 2027 block for the date, youth focus, application status and available practical details. Delete the second explanation of the event.

Keep: the focus on organisations working with young people. Cost: less standalone emphasis on the theme. Young people's participation format still needs clarification. [1,3,7]

D02

Keep one invitation. Shorten the setup.

Keep the current hero headline as the playful line. Make its explanation concrete. Replace “Good work starts with a real brief” with “How it works.” Remove “Two sides of the same team” and “Who will you be making a mark with?” above the audience cards.

Keep: equal cards, each with one reason to take part and a clear detail link. Cost: fewer campaign-style lines. The hero carries the personality. [1–2,7]

D03

Use audience pages for the decision facts.

Replace the second motivational headline with “What you commit to.” State unpaid maker time, free organisation participation, preparation and selection plainly. Explain concrete types of work before another invitation.

Keep: application buttons near the top and bottom. Repeating the action helps; repeating the pitch adds little. Do not cut planning commitments that currently appear in the forms. [8–10]

D04

Make supporting content quieter.

Give past editions one concise evidence moment. Move the BRUZZ link beside it. Put the global “organise in your city” link in the footer. Shorten About to names, short roles and contact; remove its repeated event explanation.

Keep: credit and provenance. For this cut, use the existing Brussels group image; the international hero placeholders remain clearly labelled until replaced. Cost: fewer photos in the main story. [2,4,11–12]

Selections stay local until queued. Queuing records feedback only; it does not authorise implementation.

Scope, evidence and limits

This is an editorial review of the running homepage, both audience pages and application introductions. Forms were read for commitments; field removal is outside this review. No product copy, routes, styles or forms were changed.

These are recommendations, not tested conversion improvements. Unknowns remain around venue, detailed schedule, review timing, eligible maker roles and how young people take part. The draft does not invent answers. [7]
