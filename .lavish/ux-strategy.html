Digital mission · working direction

Give Brussels social-profit organisations and creative makers a clear, trustworthy way to apply to each Make a Mark edition—and make the next step after applying easy to understand.

Working synthesis from the owner's request and five confirmed Strategy choices. User needs in the source material remain historical hypotheses.

Two application journeys; one shared outcome

Known from the owner and product records

Organisations bring a challenge and the context makers need. Makers bring skills, work examples, preferences, and availability. Airtable links both groups through teams and editions. [Forms · Airtable]

Strategy implication: the website should explain each audience's commitment in its own terms, while keeping a visible connection between the two journeys.

Review-ready applications by edition

Owner-selected North Star

Candidate measure: complete, usable maker and organisation applications received by the deadline, tracked separately by application type.

This measures the website's near-term contribution without claiming that form submissions alone create a successful match. Confirm whether “review-ready” can be measured from the current data and review process.

Who we're serving

Audience roles are ranked provisionally for this signup version. The underlying evidence is historical; current needs remain hypotheses.

Brussels social-profit organisations

Primary applicant · hypothesis

“When we have a communication or design challenge but limited capacity, help us explain it clearly and apply for a useful, realistic collaboration.”

Likely hesitation: Am I eligible? Is our challenge clear enough? Can we provide the time, assets, and people the process expects?
Evidence: application fields ask for mission, challenge, assets, and time commitment; BRUZZ reports resource constraints. [Forms · BRUZZ]

Creative makers

Primary applicant · hypothesis

“When I want to use my creative skills for a social purpose, help me understand the work, commitment, and team fit before I apply.”

Likely hesitation: Will I be a fit? What is expected before and during the day? Will the time and contribution be respected?
Evidence: BRUZZ participants describe social impact and peer connection; a maker says a first event felt overwhelming. The 2020 packet emphasizes protected, valued time. [BRUZZ · Maker Packet]

Young people aged roughly 12–25

Important stakeholder · role unresolved

The 2027 brief calls for young people's involvement in preparation and feedback, but whether they should apply as participants, makers, or feedback contributors is unsettled.

Planning stance: make their role visible in the strategy, but don't treat them as a signup audience until the owner confirms a specific role and journey.
Evidence: 2027 project brief and planning notes. [2027 brief]

Three edition moments

Keep the phase states distinct so people can tell whether they are applying now, registering future interest, or seeing what happened.

01

Signup open

Two separate applications, with clear fit, requirements, timing, and what happens after submission.

Strategic job: help each applicant decide and submit with confidence.

The supplied forms support this distinction. No exact fields are prescribed here. [Forms]

02

Signups closed

Say plainly that applications have closed. Explain what happens to submitted applications and when a new opportunity is expected, if known.

Strategic job: close the door gently and give a useful next step.

A past organisation form captures name, email, and organisation for next-cycle contact. It is historical copy, not the final experience. [Forms]

03

After the event

Recognize what participants and organisations contributed; show work and outcomes where available; offer future interest without implying everyone must continue.

Strategic job: provide a meaningful ending and an optional way back.

Local history includes showcases, thanks, and participant stories; the international guide adds follow-up/survey practices that are not local policy. [2019 recap · 2023 stories · Field Guide]

These are strategic intents, not a sitemap or screen plan. The form backend, consent, retention, phase switching, and copy belong in later decisions.

Value & channels

Proposed value proposition

For organisations: a chance to make a focused communication or design challenge clearer and more achievable with creative collaborators.
For makers: a bounded way to use professional or emerging skills for local social impact and meet peers.

The 2023 article and testimonials describe these kinds of value; they don't establish that every participant experiences it. [BRUZZ · 100PAP · BAOB]

Channel recommendation: use the website as the clear, current application destination; let direct outreach, partner networks, past participants, and communications bring people to it. Past local articles and the international guide show network and media recruitment, but current channel performance is unknown. [2019 articles · Field Guide · 2027 brief]
