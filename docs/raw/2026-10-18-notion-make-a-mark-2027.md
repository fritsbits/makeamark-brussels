## Page content

Make a Mark Brussels is described as an annual creative make-a-thon where volunteer makers help Brussels social organisations with communication, design, and digital challenges in one day. Organisations participate for free and receive concrete, usable results.

The upcoming edition is described as the ninth edition, centred on Brussels youth, on Saturday 27 March 2027. It will work with organisations serving young people around ages 12–25 and involve young people in preparation and feedback on creative proposals.

The project covers planning, organising, and managing the edition from preparation through follow-up:

- Programme and participants: select organisations, conduct intake conversations, recruit makers, and form teams.
- Practical organisation: confirm date and locations; arrange catering, photography, and the day schedule.
- Finance: manage the budget and seek sponsors and grants.
- Communications: prepare visual identity, a trilingual website, case studies, and promotion.
- Project management: maintain a reverse timeline, task allocation, meetings, and progress.

### Meeting observations and proposals

- The ninth edition retains the core idea of makers working for a day on Brussels organisations' communication challenges; outputs should be usable by the organisation.
- A reflection on the prior edition says teams that brought in members of the target audience (example: girls from Molenbeek Rebels) generated useful feedback.
- The project page sets the event date as 27 March 2027. In contrast, the meeting summary also mentions “Saturday 17 March,” with the year uncertain. The owner later confirmed 27 March 2027, resolving the date discrepancy. The meeting note says Impact House was still subject to contact and Beurscafé was reserved but not paid or confirmed.
- Strong preference was expressed for Brussels youth as the umbrella theme. Reasons recorded: broad organisational relevance, connection to current needs, focus for grant applications, and a clear communications line. Subthemes discussed included mental wellbeing, education, public space, poverty, and newcomers. These are discussion points, not a final theme decision.
- “Youth” was discussed as roughly ages 12–25. The degree and form of young people's participation remains a question: as makers, participants, and/or feedback providers. An interview or conversation with umbrella youth organisations such as Yes, Capital was suggested as input to makers.
- Estimated minimum cost was about €2,500 (€1,500 daytime catering plus €1,000 Beurscafé). In-kind support was considered more feasible than cash; Piet Kolderuit and Foodmaker were possible catering partners. Grants are uncertain, with team self-funding described as a worst case. A voluntary maker contribution (example: €20 × 50 makers = €1,000) and a multi-year structural sponsor were discussed.
- Communications proposals included Dutch/French/English website, early December launch, VRT and Bruzz as priority media, considering French-language media, identifying youth media such as Askip and Jurbi, and publishing 3–4 case studies with impact. Calling organisations for their own case-study content was preferred. A themed press release with an umbrella organisation was also considered.
- Team roles were unresolved. Griet (event manager at Urban Brussels) was considered for event coordination; a new participant was exploring communications, venues, or contacts. School of Creativity student teams (Erik) were expected to return; mixing students into teams was preferred. Social media was preferably assigned to young people or students.
- A Friday-evening briefing had been positive but costly and logistically heavy; whether to repeat it was unresolved. Maker homework was considered probably unnecessary this year. Pre-event organisation intake and an afternoon customer check-in with young people from the target group were proposed.
